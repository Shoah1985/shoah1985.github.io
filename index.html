**PART FOUR** **Scenes with Polish-speaking interviewees in Claude Lanzmann’s** ***Shoah*****.** **Transcription and translation into English** Edited by Roma Sendyka and Magda Heydel **Introductory remarks** Below is a transcription of the scenes from *Shoah* featuring speakers of Polish, along with an English translation. Each statement in Polish is followed by its English version. The exchanges between the director and the interpreters have been left in French. The material is divided into sections corresponding to the structure of the printed English edition of *Shoah* (1985), so that reading both versions side by side allows one to easily compare them and notice the extent of the omissions and alterations in Lanzmann’s version. The titles of sections follow Lanzmann’s book publication. We have made every effort to identify the individuals speaking, although in many cases it was not possible to confirm their names. In such instances, we use Lanzmann’s way of referring to particular persons or describe them by adding a distinctive feature (like a detail of clothing) to enable recognizing the speaker on screen. The order of the utterances was sometimes difficult to determine in group scenes where the speakers talk simultaneously and interrupt one another or the interpreter as well as making side comments. We tried to maintain the sequence of the utterances as strictly as possible and followed the principle of the logical flow of the conversation. The language used by Lanzmann’s interviewees is far from standard Polish, as discussed in numerous chapters in this volume. The translation endeavours to preserve these distinctive qualities even at the expense of fluency of the English version. We were particularly keen to retain specific phraseology and imagery, traces of hesitation, uncertainty and the search for the right words, as well as the variety of registers and styles of expression or structural incoherence. Phonetical peculiarities are signalled in the Polish version but not reproduced in the English text. We corrected Polish family names and toponyms misspelled by Lanzmann and introduced Polish diacritics. Endnotes were added to elucidate specific features of the bystanders’ language and to add contextual information. The following transcription and translation were made by the team: Magda Heydel, Karolina Kwaśna, Roma Sendyka, and Joanna Sobesto with the collaboration of Karolina Kamińska, Jessica Jensen Mitchell, and Natalia Roguz.
